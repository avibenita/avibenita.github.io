# Statistico video review standards

These standards are sent with every automatic review. The reviewer has no access to earlier conversations.

- Preserve Statistico's navy and cyan visual family, logo, typography, restrained camera movement, and shared components.
- Show the control, perform the real action, wait for the current results or charts, then hold the result for about 2–3 seconds where a viewer needs to read it. Avoid blank frames, loading footage, stale results, and cropped labels.
- Demonstrate real interactivity and its analytical consequence. Do not animate invented statistical values or imply a feature that the module does not perform.
- Keep narration explanatory and aligned with the action, and leave enough time to read the result.
- Keep narration stems voice-only and scene-sized. Preserve intentional silence. Keep optional music separate from narration.
- For Independent Means, keep the two silent capability cards (25 seconds and 19 seconds) and the established closing. A silence detector must not treat those cards as failed speech.
- Advertise only verified working capabilities. The capability cards have to match the current module.
- Independent Means delivery is 1728×1080, 30 fps, H.264, with fast-start. Do not treat 1920×1080 as a global requirement.
- Narration loudness stays about −16 to −17 LUFS, with true peak at or below −1 dBTP. Judge intentional silence separately. Do not boost audio that already meets this.
- Automated readiness is not publication approval. Avi views and listens to the candidate, then approves any CapCut export. The workflow must not write the approved folder or replace website assets.
- Call attention to a control or result with the shared warm-gold video overlay. Do not recolor the analytical module to match it. The module's own interface colors and behavior stay as implemented.
- The overlay frame is #FFD18A. It is a 2px outline with stronger corner brackets, placed 8–12px outside the target so it does not cover the content. Add a restrained outer glow and gently dim the surrounding area.
- Fade and draw the frame into view over about 300ms. While it is active, its outer glow breathes gently on a cycle of about 2.4 seconds. Keep the frame geometry still and the target readable. Fade it out over about 200ms. Use one attention frame at a time.
- This replaces the earlier rule of one glow pulse followed by a completely steady frame. Do not flash, bounce, or keep the border moving.
- The frame has to stay aligned with its target while the camera moves or zooms. The overlay is drawn in the recorded frame and tracked to the target's box, so a later zoom scales the target and the frame together.
- Frame sampling cannot prove a 300ms draw or a 2.4 second breath by itself. Those timings belong to the overlay constants and the highlight log. When an encoded glow sample is attached, use it. List the remaining sampling limit under unassessed checks.
- Video production is separate from formal statistical validation. A production check may confirm that a featured control changes the on-screen result. Do not claim that the module is independently validated, and do not remediate calculation defects inside the video workflow. Record those findings in the validation project.
- Build a live demonstration around a few action-response sequences. Each one shows the question, the state before the action, the action, the visible response, and a hold long enough to understand the consequence. Opening a tab or showing a static table can support that story. It should not dominate the live demonstration.
- Narration explains the relationship or consequence. For Regression, do not read coefficients, predictions, p-values, sample sizes, percentages, or R² scores aloud. Leave those values on screen. Describe a refit as a sensitivity comparison, not as proof that a higher score is a better model.
- For Regression, start narration while the opening page is visible: "Put your regression model in motion. Explore scenarios, investigate individual observations, and watch the results respond." Then a short card, "Regression" and "Explore the model," separates the introduction from the live interface. Leave a brief narration pause on that card. Do not leave a long silent logo. Use a brief chapter cue only when the analytical question changes.
- Do not feature a Regression scene whose claim depends on a known calculation defect. Report any capability the film does not demonstrate because the module does not support it.
- These Regression narration and story rules apply when the film is Regression. Other modules keep their own opening and, until they are revised, their existing narrated results. Independent Means keeps its two silent capability cards.

Sampling is not a complete motion review. A transcript does not prove that the voice sounds natural, that pronunciation is correct, that music or noise is absent, or that transitions feel pleasant. Those points stay on the human pass unless a later check is actually implemented and named in the report.
