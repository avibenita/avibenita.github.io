How to read this view

Expected-count checks plus a residual map that shows which cells depart from independence.

What each cell is

Rows are categories of the row variable; columns are categories of the column variable. Each tile is one combination (for example, rating 3 in Israel).

The two numbers

The large number is the adjusted residual (Haberman): how far that cell is from the count expected if the two variables were independent, in standard-error units. The smaller line is observed / expected — the actual count versus the independence prediction. Hover a cell for percents and the residual.

Color

Orange = more cases than expected in that cell. Blue = fewer than expected. Gray = close to expected. Darker color means a larger |residual|.

How large is “large”?

|r| < 2 — typical sampling noise (neutral).
|r| 2–3 — notable; worth naming in the write-up.
|r| ≥ 3 — strong departure from independence.
|r| ≈ 1.96 is roughly an uncorrected 5% test for that cell. With many cells, treat a single |r| just over 2 as a hint, not a confirmed finding.

How to interpret a pattern

Look for a block of orange and blue, not one isolated cell. Example: if rating 3 is orange in the Netherlands and blue in Israel, that rating is over-represented in one country and under-represented in the other — that contrast is what χ² is picking up. The Overview p-value says whether an association exists; this map says where.

The cards above the map

Cells E < 5 — share of cells whose expected count is below 5. Chi-square p-values get shaky when this is over about 20%.
Minimum E — smallest expected count. Below 1 is especially unreliable.
Cells E < 1 — how many cells are that sparse. For a 2×2 table, prefer Fisher’s exact test when counts are small.
The yellow banner repeats this warning when the approximation is weak.

Heat map vs Values
