t / p it (or z) and p-value for the pooled effect. With Hartung–Knapp, a t test with k−1 df is used; otherwise a Wald z test. Small p suggests the average differs from 0.

I² iI² — share of total variation across studies that is due to heterogeneity rather than sampling error. Rough guide: <25% low, 25–50% moderate, >50% substantial. I² = 0% does not prove homogeneity.

τ² iτ² (tau-squared) — estimated between-study variance of true effects under a random-effects model. τ = √τ² is the between-study SD on the effect scale. Near 0 means little excess dispersion.

Heterogeneity Statistics
Q iCochran’s Q — weighted sum of squared deviations of study effects from the pooled mean. Large Q relative to df suggests between-study heterogeneity.
—
df iDegrees of freedom for Q, typically k − 1 (number of studies minus one).
—
p (Q) ip-value for Q under the null of homogeneous true effects. Small p suggests excess dispersion; with few studies power is limited.
—
I² iI² — percent of variability across studies attributable to heterogeneity rather than chance. Does not prove studies are homogeneous when near 0.
—
H² iH² = Q / df. Values near 1 suggest little excess heterogeneity; larger values indicate more between-study dispersion.
—
τ² / τ iτ² is between-study variance; τ = √τ² is the between-study SD on the effect scale. Both are 0 under a fixed-effect model.
