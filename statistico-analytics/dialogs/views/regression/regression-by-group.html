Similarity Profile™

How to read Similarity Profile™ for regression coefficients by group.

This is a practical, non-academic approach original to Statistico. It is not a published coefficient, not a p-value, and not a substitute for a Chow test or interaction test. Use it to describe how alike group coefficient structures look in applied work.

What it is for

After you split the same regression by a grouping column, Similarity Profile™ asks: which groups share the same coefficient structure, and which stand apart? It compares every pair of groups (Overall omitted) on pattern, strength, and sign, then rates them from 0 to 100.

The three components

Pattern — do the same predictors pull in the same relative directions? Tucker congruence of the aligned β-vectors (intercept omitted).
Strength — are coefficients similarly large in magnitude (mean |β|)?
Sign — do predictors agree on direction?
Overall combines the three with a geometric mean, so one weak component is not hidden by two strong ones.

How to read the scores

Group homogeneity is the overall index: the geometric mean of every pairwise Overall score (Overall group omitted). A higher index may indicate greater similarity between groups in coefficient pattern, strength, and sign.
Comparisons use the four gauge ranges, not small gaps between scores:
90–100 Very similar
75–89 Mostly similar
50–74 Mixed similarity
Below 50 Substantially different
Most similar / most distinct / most different are only named when pairs or groups fall in different ranges. Click a table row for the three-component profile.

What it is not

It does not test whether two regressions differ significantly. Treat these scores as an effect-size style description of practical similarity, designed here for Statistico. Terms that do not vary inside a group are skipped.
