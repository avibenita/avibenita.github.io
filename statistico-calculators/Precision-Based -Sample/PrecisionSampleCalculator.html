Assumptions and help

The margin of error is half the confidence-interval width. A margin of ±5 means the interval runs from 5 below the estimate to 5 above it, so the width is 10.

The mean method uses a z calculation with a known standard deviation. If that standard deviation comes from earlier data, treat it as a planning assumption, not as a value this study has already measured. NIST engineering statistics handbook.

The proportion method is a normal approximation. Be cautious with a small sample or with a proportion near 0% or 100%. NIST engineering statistics handbook.

Both difference methods assume two independent groups of equal size. The sample size shown is the size of each group.

How this calculator works

The left side is what you enter. The centered card is the result. The result changes as soon as you edit a valid field. There is no separate calculate step.

Sample size. You choose the margin of error you can accept. The result is how many observations that precision requires.

Margin of error. You enter a sample size you already have. The result is the margin of error that sample can support.

The picture under the result is the confidence interval. The dot is the estimate. Each end is one margin of error away, so the distance from end to end is the interval width. Drag Confidence level to widen or narrow that interval: a higher confidence uses a larger critical value and needs more data for the same margin.

The chart uses the same margin formula as the result card. Its horizontal line is the target margin when you are solving for sample size, and the calculated margin when you start from a sample size.
